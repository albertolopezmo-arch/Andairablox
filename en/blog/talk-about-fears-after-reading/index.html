How to talk about fears after reading a story

Alberto López

Stories can invite a gentle conversation about feelings; they are not a substitute for professional help with persistent fears.

Start with the character

Ask what seems to worry the character and what clues the child noticed. Avoid immediately asking about the child’s own fears. Talking about fiction may feel less personal and gives them room to decide what to share.

Explore choices without judgment

Imagine two ways the character could ask for help and who might support them. Listen without labeling choices brave or cowardly. If the child shares a personal worry, acknowledge it and avoid pressing for details.

Let them choose what comes next

Offer a break or another chapter. If fear regularly disrupts sleep, school or daily activities, seek advice from a qualified healthcare professional. Reading can support conversation, but a novel is not a treatment.
